Cyclistic — Turning casual riders into annual members

Google Data Analytics Capstone · Case Study 1

Business task: Cyclistic's finance team has concluded that annual members are far more profitable than casual riders. The marketing director wants to know how casual riders and annual members use Cyclistic bikes differently, so a campaign can be designed to convert casual riders into members — rather than pursue new-customer marketing.

Rides by day of week, member vs casual

What the data shows

  • Casual riders take roughly twice the trip length of members (~26 min vs. ~12 min average), consistent with leisure use rather than point-to-point commuting.
  • Casual ridership spikes on weekends; member ridership stays flat Mon–Fri with clear AM/PM commute peaks — a strong signal that members ride to get somewhere, casual riders ride for the ride.
  • Casual usage concentrates in summer months (Jun–Aug), while member usage holds steadier year-round.
  • Classic/docked bikes skew casual; electric bike usage is closer to an even split between the two groups.

Recommendations

  1. Launch a "weekday commuter trial" — a discounted first month of membership targeted at casual riders whose ride history shows weekday, short-duration trips (early conversion signal).
  2. Run a summer-only digital campaign at the docking stations with the highest casual traffic, timed to the June–August usage spike.
  3. Add an in-app prompt after a rider's 3rd casual ride in a rolling 30-day window, showing a personalized "you'd have saved $X with a membership" calculator.

Bellabeat — What smart-device data says about daily habits

Google Data Analytics Capstone · Case Study 2

Business task: Bellabeat's co-founder wants to understand how consumers already use non-Bellabeat smart devices, so those insights can shape marketing strategy for one Bellabeat product — here, the Leaf wellness tracker.

What the data shows

  • Average daily steps sit around 7,600 — below the commonly cited 10,000-step benchmark, suggesting most users have room for a gentle activity nudge, not an aggressive one.
  • Sedentary time dominates the day — roughly 16–17 hours average, against only ~21 minutes of "very active" time.
  • Sleep logging is inconsistent — well under half of user-days include a sleep entry, meaning it's a feature people own but don't habitually use.
  • Activity is highest midweek (Tue–Wed) and dips on weekends — the opposite pattern from Cyclistic's casual riders, a useful contrast when framing weekday vs. weekend campaigns.

Recommendations

  1. Add short "movement break" push notifications during long sedentary stretches detected by the Leaf, rather than a single daily step goal.
  2. Build a low-friction sleep-logging flow (auto-detect + one-tap confirm) since sleep tracking is the most under-used feature relative to its wellness value.
  3. Run a weekend-specific engagement challenge to counter the drop-off in logged activity on Saturdays and Sundays.

TFM Xpress — What overflow coverage really costs the day

Original case study · 3-tonne delivery driver, TFM Xpress, Perth WA

Business task: My KPI is 35 deliveries a day — mostly commercial/warehouse drops (JB Hi-Fi, Big W and similar) plus a handful of residential houses — across a fixed core route through Belmont, Morley, Bayswater, Dianella and the surrounding inner north-eastern suburbs. Some days the core route comfortably clears 35 on its own; other days it falls short and I fill up in the outer Swan Valley suburbs — Ellenbrook, Aveley, Caversham and similar. I wanted to know what that overflow coverage actually costs in time and distance, and whether it lines up with the "busy day / quiet day" pattern I already feel on the road.

What the data shows

  • Across 130 workdays and 4,627 stops (6 months, weekdays only, 3-tonne truck, flat $33/hour), the 35-delivery KPI was hit every single day.
  • Overflow coverage into the Swan Valley suburbs was needed on 57% of workdays (74 of 130) — but it is almost entirely a mid-week problem, not a random one.
  • Monday and Friday rarely need overflow at all (11.5% and 15.4% of days) — those are naturally the highest-volume core days, so the regular route clears the KPI on its own.
  • Wednesday and Thursday need overflow on more than 96% of days, with Tuesday sitting in between at 65% — core volume is simply quieter mid-week, so the outer suburbs carry the slack almost every time.
  • Overflow days cost about 1.1 extra hours and 64 extra km to deliver essentially the same number of stops: 8.80 hours / 176.1 km on overflow days vs. 7.68 hours / 112.5 km on core-only days, for 35.0 vs. 36.4 stops respectively.
  • Distance efficiency drops sharply once overflow kicks in — 5.03 km between stops vs. 3.09 km/stop on the core route, since Ellenbrook, Aveley, The Vines, Brigadoon and Caversham are simply much further apart than the inner suburbs.
  • Belmont, Morley, Bayswater and Dianella are the busiest core suburbs by far (visited on 77, 70, 69 and 67 of 130 days) — consistent with Belmont and Morley being home to Belmont Forum and the Galleria, where JB Hi-Fi and Big W volume concentrates.

Recommendations

  1. Treat Wednesday and Thursday as "overflow-guaranteed" days rather than a maybe — pre-plan an efficient Swan Valley leg the night before instead of deciding reactively once the core route runs dry.
  2. When overflow is needed, prioritise the nearest suburbs (Caversham, Aveley) before the furthest (Upper Swan, Herne Hill) to limit the km/stop blowout on those days.
  3. Since Monday and Friday almost never need overflow, that's the time to fit in anything else time-sensitive (vehicle checks, admin, proof-of-delivery cleanup) without risking the KPI.
